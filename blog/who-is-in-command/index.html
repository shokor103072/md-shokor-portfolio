If ASI Goes to War, Who Is Really in Command?

When decisions move faster than human understanding, is final approval the same as meaningful control?

Md Shokor A Rahaman·30 September 2026

Imagine a future war in which the most consequential decisions are made by an intelligence whose reasoning no human can fully understand. Who bears responsibility for that war?

The president who gave final approval? The general who issued the command? The engineer who built the system? Or the artificial superintelligence—ASI—that made thousands of decisions in milliseconds, following a chain of reasoning no person could completely trace?

There is an even more uncomfortable question. If a human can press APPROVE at the end of a process but cannot genuinely verify why a decision was made, is that human still in control? Or are we holding on to an illusion of control?

A competition that could outpace us

For those of us researching AI, it is becoming difficult to ignore how quickly the field is changing. I am not saying that the United States, China, or anyone else has already achieved ASI. But as frontier systems from OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepSeek, Kimi, and others develop in coding, reasoning, autonomous agents, research, and tool use, a question naturally follows: what happens if this competition eventually reaches ASI?

Consider a hypothetical future in which both the United States and China have ASI systems able to analyse and respond across defence, cybersecurity, the economy, logistics, energy, and intelligence far faster than humans can.

One day, a conflict begins. The US system reports a threat and recommends an immediate response. Within milliseconds, the Chinese system anticipates that response and develops a counterstrategy. The US system predicts the counterstrategy. The Chinese system counters again.

ASI → counter-ASI → counter-counter-ASI → …

Within seconds, thousands of strategic decisions might be proposed. What do the humans see? Perhaps only a succession of prompts: APPROVE? APPROVE? APPROVE?

Authority is not understanding

Technically, humans would still have control because a person gives the final approval. But the power to approve a decision and the ability to evaluate it are different things. If the entire decision chain becomes faster and more complex than human comprehension, how real is that control?

I think here of a hadith narrated by Abu Huraira, in which the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ describes a time when the killer does not know why he has killed and the victim does not know why they have been killed. Sahih Muslim 2908a.

I am not claiming that this hadith is about AI or ASI. But the image invites reflection. Imagine one machine making a decision, another machine making a counterdecision, and the first responding again—at a speed that gradually reduces the human role from decision-maker to approval layer. At the end of that chain, people are dying, while no individual human can fully explain why this particular outcome occurred.

Who is truly in command?

An ASI-versus-ASI conflict might not look like a Hollywood battle between robots. Presidents would still issue orders. Generals would still hold authority. Engineers would monitor systems. Humans would still sit in the control room. Yet the strategic interaction could take place at a level faster than human understanding.

That leads me to the deepest question: if humans start a war, but the next thousand decisions are made by intelligences they cannot fully understand, is it still a human war? Or are humans becoming the legal and moral approval layer for a conflict understood primarily by machines?

If the intelligence conducting a war understands it better than the humans supposedly controlling it, who is truly in command?
